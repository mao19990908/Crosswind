# sae-730232 — Hucho and Emmelmann 1973

## Pass 1: location

Core aerodynamic-derivative theory between steady-yaw wind-tunnel coefficients and spatially graded crosswind input. Original SAE paper pp. 892–900 checked on 2026-09-25. It explicitly cites the 1967 Beauvais experiment in reference 3 and discusses its yaw-moment overshoot (p. 899).

## Pass 2: input, model, output and limit

- Input: a spatial crosswind transition through a protected-to-exposed road region. The example uses 27.8 m/s vehicle speed, 16 m/s final crosswind, 30° final relative yaw and mixing-zone length ratios 1–5 (pp. 897–898, Figs. 10–11). A separate time-varying-at-all-positions gust class is identified but not numerically explored here.
- Method: slender-body potential-flow estimate with side-contour flat-plate approximation, mirror ground and approximate underbody correction. It produces steady and transient side-force/yaw-moment coefficients, while missing rear-shape distinctions (pp. 894–897).
- Evidence: comparison with full-scale wind-tunnel data applies to **steady** zero-yaw coefficient gradients only. The predicted transient overshoot weakens as the wind gradient smooths; Fig. 12's larger lateral displacement is a modeled vehicle response. The authors explicitly state their own transient comparison measurements were unfinished (pp. 897–899).
- Boundary: no experimental Level B transient-load validation or Level C road-response validation in this paper. Closed-loop driver control is mentioned as a future/motivating analysis, not an experimentally verified driver-in-loop result. Do not read the theory's gradient result as measured aerodynamic admittance.
