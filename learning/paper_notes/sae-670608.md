# sae-670608 — Beauvais 1967

## Pass 1: location

Core historical moving-model wind-tunnel experiment between the early road-gust work and later transient-load methods. It asks when steady-yaw aerodynamic measurements can stand in for a vehicle entering a lateral wind region. Full SAE paper pp. 2219–2225 checked on 2026-09-25.

## Pass 2: input, method, output and boundary

- Input: a 1:10 Ford Galaxie crosses a steady tunnel airstream on a perpendicular track. This is a finite spatial crosswind encounter, not a time-varying fan gust at a fixed model and not a freely responding road vehicle.
- Method: front/rear strain-gauge reactions give side force and yaw moment. Static yaw tests provide a matched comparator. The authors measured tunnel entry profiles, checked the balance daily and timed carriage passage with track switches; see source record and pp. 2220–2223.
- Output: normalized side force and yaw moment versus penetration distance in car lengths. Most traces reach a near-steady plateau by about three lengths; plateau values check within 5% of the steady comparator. Side force builds gradually; yaw moment can overshoot when front force develops ahead of rear force (pp. 2223–2224, Figs. 7–9).
- Conclusion: the author regards quasi-steady data as adequate through 15° relative yaw for the tested conditions, with noticeable transient yaw-moment effects generally above that angle (pp. 2224–2225, Fig. 10). Do not generalize the 15° boundary to other shapes, gust edges, or vehicle dynamics.
- Limits: no freely moving vehicle, driver, road wind, independent transient-load validation, uncertainty budget or raw digital signals. The 18 ft/s example in Fig. 7 differs from the stated 20–40 ft/s dynamic model-speed range; preserve as unresolved.
