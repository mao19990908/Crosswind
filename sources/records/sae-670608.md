## Full-text review: Pass 1–2 (2026-09-25)

- The complete seven-page SAE paper (printed pp. 2219–2225) was read, including Figs. 7–10. `verification_status=full_text_verified`; Pass 3 reproduction audit remains open. The receipt-stage verification note above is historical and superseded.
- **Input and setup:** a 1:10 1962 Ford Galaxie model traverses the University of Michigan wind-tunnel test section on a track perpendicular to steady tunnel flow. This creates a finite, spatial crosswind encounter for the moving model, not a natural-road gust or a moving vehicle dynamics test. Tunnel section: 5 × 7 × 25 ft; 25-ft model track; model length 1.646 ft. Front/rear strain-gauge reactions give side force and yaw moment (pp. 2220–2222, Figs. 1–6).
- **Calibration and comparator:** tunnel velocity profiles were measured near both opening edges at model height; the entry boundary layer is reported as approximately 6 in. Balance checked at least daily; carriage speed from three position switches. Static yaw tests cover 0–90° in 5° steps at 20–70 ft/s tunnel speed. Dynamic tests are described as 20–40 ft/s model speed and 20–80 ft/s tunnel speed (p. 2222). Fig. 7 labels an example as 18 ft/s model speed, below the stated test range; this discrepancy is unresolved.
- **Result and mechanism:** after approximately three vehicle lengths of penetration, steady portions of most dynamic force traces agree within 5% of steady predictions; that is a matched internal comparison, not an independent transient validation. Side-force buildup is gradual. Yaw moment can temporarily overshoot its eventual steady value because the front reaction rises before the rear reaction, moving the effective pressure center forward while the vehicle straddles the gust edge (pp. 2223–2224, Figs. 7–9). Fig. 10 and the conclusion place noticeable yaw-moment transient effects generally above 15° relative yaw for this setup; the author considers quasi-steady data adequate up to 15° in the tested conditions (pp. 2224–2225). This is not a universal vehicle-stability threshold.
- **Evidence boundary:** local wind-profile and balance calibration support facility and load measurement checks, but no independent dynamic-load reference or moving vehicle/driver response is shown. Exact repeat count, uncertainty budget and digitized raw time series are not reported. `open_loop` aerodynamic model test; Level C vehicle-response validation is absent.
- **Historical note:** the author says the material was first presented at the 1966 FISITA Munich congress (p. 2225). Treat this as an earlier presentation of the same work until the proceedings are checked, not as a separately validated study.
