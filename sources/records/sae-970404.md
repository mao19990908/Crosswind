## Full-text Pass 2 review (2026-09-25)

- `verification_status=full_text_verified`; old abstract-only status is superseded. All 18 PDF pages read; article pp.141–156 and Figs.7–21 checked. This is moving-boundary aerodynamic CFD of (a) a one-box car entering a modeled lateral wind zone and (b) two one-box cars passing. Neither branch solves tire/driver/vehicle motion.
- Crosswind branch prescribes 27.8 m/s vehicle speed and 13 m/s perpendicular crossflow, while article p.146 calls their composite yaw about 28°. Direct arithmetic `atan(13/27.8)=25.1°`; the discrepancy is unresolved and could reflect a local effective velocity not specified there. A full-scale one-box model, ~250,000 elements, 53×26×5.5 m domain and nominal nine-vehicle-length wind zone are described (article pp.142–143). Sliding discontinuous interface plus ALE mesh movement, k–ε turbulence, third-order MUSCL reverting to first-order near overshoots, wall-function region `y+≈100–500`; tire rotation omitted. Mesh convergence and independent wind-field uncertainty are not reported.
- In computed crosswind entry, yaw moment peaks around `X/L≈1.4` (position datum defined after diffusive wind onset), while total side force increases without a comparable peak. Front leeward and windward blocks dominate yaw buildup (pp.145–147, Figs.7–13). A crosswind spoiler reduces simulated yaw moment ~20%; article Fig.17 p.149 separately plots an *actual* vehicle yaw-rate reduction over 20% in its wind generator. The latter supports engineering direction but does not directly verify the CFD yaw-moment waveform or pressure distribution. A section of their validation comparison is only qualitative.
- Passing branch uses 22.2 m/s leading and 33.3 m/s passing vehicles (11.1 m/s relative), ~270,000 cells; dynamic CFD predicts the leading car yaw moment near `X/L≈0.5` almost twice a static two-car calculation (pp.142–150, Fig.20). External experiment shown in Fig.19 uses a *large bus* passing a one-box car, and Fig.21 cites a station wagon passed by a bus; these differ from the computed two one-box geometry. Their agreement is qualitative in peak location and cannot quantify error on the modeled passing pair. Passing is a separate aerodynamic disturbance class, not a roadside wind gust.
- Reference list p.156 cites SAE 881878 for one-box crosswind and SAE 700037 for passing-bus aerodynamic load; neither is a current canonical source. Full reproducibility of the moving-interface interpolation, wall-model and force traces remains unverified; current Pass 2 target is complete.
