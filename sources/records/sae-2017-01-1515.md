## Physical problem and method
- Overset-mesh CFD drives vehicle through prescribed event modeled after ISO 12021:2010; unfiltered force/moment histories feed MBD.
- Vehicle attitude does not feed back to CFD. Compared with quasi-steady yaw-polar curve fits.
- Three vehicles: Ford Escort sedan, Everest SUV, B-Max.

## Results / validation / evidence
- One-way method predicts yaw rate, roll rate and lateral acceleration within physical-test variability; quasi-steady method underpredicts response for all three cases in abstract.
- Validation: Level C compared with full-scale test; Level A/B details not reported in abstract.

## Limitations / relevance / follow-up
- Limitation: no motion feedback; ISO-like event not natural road gust.
- Relevance: strong Level 2 engineering-validation node.
- Follow-up: extract mesh/timestep, input profile, coordinate transformation, synchronization and test uncertainty.

## Full-text Pass 2 and Pass 3 boundary review (2026-09-25)

- `verification_status=full_text_verified`; preceding abstract-only statements are superseded. All 15 PDF pages read; article pp.3–13 and Figs.4–23/Table 2 checked. Lommel fan gust: physical yaw ~20°, length ~16.6 m (~3 car lengths), nominal frequency 2 Hz. Three full-scale vehicles are tested with steering held fixed. At least six passes each; yaw/roll rate and lateral acceleration sampled at 50 Hz with anti-alias filter. Wind-off control runs remove road-induced baseline; yaw-polar comparator uses steady full-scale wind-tunnel data (pp.3–4).
- Facility wind characterization was from 2002 and authors acknowledge possible drift by the 2017 study. Both measured and simulated along-track velocities vary about 20% across the gust; CFD plug fan profiles omit swirl, housing and road camber, leading to sharper edge mixing. Above ~1 m height, vertical CFD/measurement profiles diverge; no independent same-day flow-field validation is shown (p.4, Figs.4–5).
- One-way workflow uses STAR-CCM+ v9.04.009 SST k-ω DES, overset vehicle moving through modeled fan array, timestep `2.5×10^-4 s`, ~1.5M outer plus ~32M inner cells. **No grid-dependence study**; vehicle translation through prescribed path is represented but gust-induced attitude/lateral displacement does not feed back to CFD. The six unfiltered load histories feed ADAMS/Chassis MBD as time splines (pp.5–8). The quasi-steady comparator uses yaw-polar lookup plus an empirical wind-speed factor `0.65<k<0.8`, so it is not an unmodified steady-yaw model.
- Figs.21–23 show one-way predictions generally inside measured pass-to-pass spread for yaw, roll and lateral acceleration, while this particular tuned quasi-steady comparator underpredicts yaw in the three cases and Everest roll/lateral acceleration by ~40%. **Table 2 p.13 shows both methods' lateral deviation estimates within one measured SD for all three**: Everest measured 0.92±0.15 m, one-way 0.92 m vs quasi-steady 0.77 m; Escort 1.05±0.23 m, one-way 1.19 m vs quasi-steady 1.09 m; B-Max 1.20±0.21 m, one-way 1.21 m vs quasi-steady 1.23 m. Hence one-way improvement depends on response metric and body style; no general superiority for lane displacement.
- Article p.8 cites Hucho/Emmelmann `sae-730232` as a different steep-gradient case where simply applying steady derivatives could overpredict; no contradiction with this case's *scaled* quasi-steady underprediction. Direct references also include Klein/Hogue `sae-800848` and ISO 12021:2010. The ISO event is approximated, not evidence of natural road wind equivalence. A full Pass 3 sign/frame and time-spline replay would require original inputs and is still pending.
