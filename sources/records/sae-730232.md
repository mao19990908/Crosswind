## Full-text review: Pass 1–2 (2026-09-25)

- The complete nine-page SAE paper (printed pp. 892–900) was read, including the model, the 30° example, the limits and references. `verification_status=full_text_verified`; mathematical Pass 3 reconstruction remains open. The receipt-stage verification note above is historical and superseded.
- **Input and theory:** the authors adapt slender-body potential-flow theory to a flat plate representing the projected side contour. They use a ground mirror image, neglect vehicle thickness in the first approximation and correct the excluded underbody region approximately. This cannot distinguish important rear-shape differences and is intended as an engineering estimate, not an exact solution (pp. 893–897).
- **Steady comparator:** gradients of side-force and yaw-moment coefficients at zero yaw are compared with full-scale VW wind-tunnel results. Side-force agreement is described as fairly good, with larger deviations for the Beetle; yaw-moment deviations are larger for the Beetle and Squareback. This is steady-coefficient comparison, not validation of transient gust loads (p. 897, Fig. 9).
- **Transient input/output:** the paper separates a time-varying wind at every road position from a spatial wind gradient fixed in the environment, then calculates one spatial mixing-zone profile. Example: vehicle speed 27.8 m/s, fully developed crosswind 16 m/s, final relative yaw 30°, and mixing-zone length/vehicle length 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 (pp. 897–898, Figs. 10–11). Predicted side-force and yaw-moment rises become steeper as the gradient steepens; overshoot disappears for smoother profiles. A vehicle lateral-deviation calculation with the shortest gradient gives a larger deviation than steady-coefficient assumptions (p. 899, Fig. 12). This is model output, not road-test validation.
- **Primary citation and limitation:** reference 3 explicitly cites Beauvais SAE 670608, and the text says Beauvais measured a similar overshoot in yaw moment only; the authors' own transient comparison measurements were not complete (p. 899). Record a `references` edge, but do not infer direct inheritance of the 1967 apparatus or equations. The paper discusses a vehicle/driver/road closed loop as a motivation, but the cited calculation does not establish a validated driver-in-loop experiment.
- **Pass 3 open:** inspect formula typography visually, recover the exact potential/pressure expressions, reference axes and coefficient normalization, and reproduce at least one Fig. 11/12 curve. OCR omits many equations; no formula should be reconstructed from OCR fragments.
