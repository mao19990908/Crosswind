## Full-text structured review — 2026-09-25 (supersedes abstract-only status above)

- `verification_status=full_text_verified`; all nine PDF pages read, with Figs. 3–8 and the repeatability table visually checked. `access_level=full_text`. This is an open-loop aerodynamic model test, not a freely moving/driver-controlled vehicle.
- **Research question / input:** whether fixed-yaw coefficients reproduce loads on a model that *moves* through the Cranfield tunnel crossflow. The reported comparison uses a **non-skewed, smooth-flow** gust. Boundary-layer skew and turbulence-scale modelling are explicitly deferred (PDF pp. 4, 7–9); the authors' expectation for skewed flow is a future hypothesis, not a measured result.
- **Vehicle / facility:** three lightweight generic squareback, notchback and fastback models. A 24 m transverse track crosses a 2.4 m × 1.2 m boundary-layer tunnel; 15 m upstream fetch is described. The carriage can coast at up to 18 m/s; presented runs are about 12 m/s. A five-component strain-gauge balance measures front/rear lift and side force plus pitch/yaw/roll moments (PDF pp. 4–5, Figs. 2–3). Exact model scale and gust-edge thickness are not reported in full text.
- **Coordinate / normalization:** paper Fig. 3 defines an axis system mirrored through x–z from MIRA, so positive yaw gives positive side force. The reported data use the ground-plane reference, translated from a balance center near mid-wheelbase 75 mm above the pan. Drag was not measured, so pitch coefficient at the translated reference cannot be calculated (p. 5). Force coefficients use dynamic pressure and frontal area; moments additionally use wheelbase (p. 3 notation). Do not transfer sign to other papers without transforming axes.
- **Data reduction / validation:** carriage position/speed comes from track LEDs and a photoelectric cell. Acquisition hardware permits **up to** 5 kHz, not a confirmed rate for each plotted run. Figure 4 zeroes, calibrates and filters the balance signal; wind-off/zero-yaw position-aligned subtraction removes a track signature. Fig. 5 defines peak and fully immersed mean over “tail in” to “nose out” (pp. 5–6). Nine-repeat standard deviations are reported at 0° and 17°: for example C_Y 0.007/0.011 and C_Mz 0.001/0.002 (p. 6 table). These quantify local repeatability, not a complete error budget or independent transient-load validation.
- **Results:** lift divergence for some configurations begins near 15° (Fig. 7); side force stays similar until about 30° then the static gradient weakens while dynamic values rise (p. 8 Fig. 8). For yaw moment, peak/mean/static are close through 15°, then peak dynamic moment exceeds steady for all three models, most strongly notchback; mean dynamic moment stays close to steady for squareback and fastback (p. 8 Fig. 8). Therefore the abstract's broad “dynamic forces and moments larger above 15°” must be read component- and metric-specifically. The 15° boundary is not a universal crosswind limit. Reynolds number is reported as 3.4×10^5 at 0° to 4.7×10^5 at 45° (p. 7); no blockage correction was applied.
- **CFD / dynamics / coupling:** not applicable. No measured vehicle motion or driver response. `validation_level=internal_consistency` for static/dynamic within this apparatus only.
- **Limitations / relation:** simple shapes, smooth non-skewed gust, uncorrected blockage, potential inertial/track artifacts, and no external transient reference. Reference 8 on p. 9 cites Beauvais/Brüning 1966 FISITA presentation; this might be a version relation to SAE 670608 but the conference original is not yet verified. Ryan/Dominy 1998 explicitly cites this paper as reference 13 and later challenges its 30° yaw-moment comparison under different apparatus/measurement choices.
- **Evidence map:** pp. 4–5 Figs. 2–4 facility/axes/reduction; p. 6 Figs. 5–6 and table filtering and repeatability; pp. 7–8 Figs. 7–8 component- and peak/mean-specific comparisons; p. 9 summary/limits/references. No quantitative interpolation from the graph has been entered without digitization.
