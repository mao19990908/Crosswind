## Full-text structured review — 2026-09-25 (supersedes abstract-only status above)

- `verification_status=full_text_verified`; all eleven PDF pages read, including Figs. 1–12, conclusion, references and coefficient definitions. This is an open-loop stationary **model** in a moving lateral-gust field, not a freely moving road car.
- **Research question / input:** a 0°–30°–0° relative-yaw pulse is generated by a second wind-tunnel jet behind a moving belt/aperture. Axial jet, aperture belt and axial component of the side jet are about 9 m/s; the cross component is about 0.58 times the nominal forward speed. A 1.5 m aperture creates a pulse of roughly **three 0.5 m vehicle lengths**, with finite mixed edges (PDF pp. 4–5, Figs. 1–3). The model is 2.00 m downstream of the main jet exit per prior facility survey. This is a controlled jet pulse, not a natural stochastic road gust.
- **Geometry / setup:** generic hatchback akin to an Ahmed body; fixed ground board, 28%-of-model-height clearance, no moving ground. Reynolds number about 3×10^5. There are 57 taps on each side and 100 on the upper surface (214 overall); lower surface is uninstrumented. Five fast transducers measure pressures, with an optical belt trigger; nominal acquisition 2400 Hz per channel for ~1.6 s/run. Two apertures per run are averaged and a three-point neighbour average smooths the data (pp. 5–7, Figs. 4–5). Run-to-run count beyond those apertures is not reported in full text.
- **Normalization / loads:** paper's definitions on PDF p. 11 give C_Y=Y/(½ρAv²), C_mz=M_z/(½ρAv²l), upper-surface-only C_zu=Z_u/(½ρAv²), and C_p=(p-p∞)/(p₀∞-p∞), with zero-yaw frontal area A and model length l. Pressure integration yields loads; upper-surface lift is **not whole-car lift** because the underbody is unmeasured. Exact yaw-moment reference point for comparison should be rechecked against its pressure-integration geometry before transferring magnitudes across studies.
- **Comparison / results:** steady comparator is the same model rotated to 0° and 30° in the **main jet**, while transient gust uses the second jet/belt (p. 6). Net side-force peak C_Y≈2.0 versus steady 1.70, **18% higher**; upper-surface lift peak C_zu≈1.61 versus steady 1.44, **12% higher** (p. 7 Fig. 9). Net yaw-moment peak C_mz≈0.37 versus steady 0.39, so it does **not** show corresponding total yaw overshoot; only the *windward-side contribution* has an early ~40% overshoot relative to its later value (p. 8 Fig. 10). These different denominators and components must not be merged.
- **Flow mechanism:** windward side adapts rapidly; leeward pressure field and upper-surface field lag the pulse and do not fully attain steady-30° patterns while fully immersed (pp. 8–9 Figs. 11–12). The 2-D model's large leeward pressure overshoot from Docton is absent in this 3-D hatchback, plausibly due to crossflow relief as argued by the authors (p. 10); this mechanism is interpretation, not an isolated controlled shape comparison here.
- **Validation / uncertainty:** facility input was previously surveyed with a five-hole probe; this paper compares transient to internal steady data. Coefficient of error is 0.80% for steady and 7.50% for transient (pp. 7–8). No independent transient force balance or vehicle-response validation; Level A is referenced facility work, Level B is internal pressure comparison, Level C none. The paper itself notes belt/tunnel asymmetry and post-gust nonzero side force, finite tap resolution, smoothing and fixed ground.
- **CFD / vehicle dynamics / coupling:** not applicable; no CFD run or driver/controller model in this paper.
- **Relation to previous work:** PDF p. 11 references Beauvais 1967 (`sae-670608`, ref. 2) and Macklin/Garry/Howell 1996 (`sae-960674`, ref. 13). P. 10 explicitly disputes transfer of the Macklin 30° yaw-moment result to this facility, citing peak-vs-mean, balance/track dynamics and different gust generation as possible explanations; none is independently isolated. This is a *conditional finding conflict*, not a general invalidation of the 1996 experiment.
- **Evidence map:** pp. 4–5 Figs. 1–5 input/facility/taps; pp. 6–7 Figs. 6–9 steady comparator and side/lift coefficients; pp. 8–9 Figs. 10–12 yaw/pressure evolution; p. 10 conditional cross-study critique and conclusions; p. 11 references/normalizations.
